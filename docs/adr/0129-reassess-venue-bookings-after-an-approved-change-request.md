# ADR-0129: An approved change request reassesses what a direct edit reassesses

- **Status:** Accepted; the rules in SCRUM-58's criteria and table were agreed with the Product Owner (ADR-0094's answers of 25 Oct, the rewrite of 29 Oct), and leaving a booking On Hold out (decision 10) is the Product Owner's decision of 2 Nov, recorded on SCRUM-58; refusing an invalid approval (decision 2), decisions 3 to 6, the mock's reading of a Confirmed event (decision 8) and the second-rise rule (decision 9) are defaults to review
- **Date:** 2026-11-02
- **Story:** SCRUM-58 Reassess venue bookings after an approved change request
- **Extends:** [ADR-0094](0094-reassess-each-venue-arrangement-after-a-significant-change.md) (the flags and who is told), [ADR-0087](0087-withdrawn-safety-approval-returns-the-event-to-approved.md) (a withdrawn approval sends the event back), [ADR-0107](0107-tell-the-safety-officer-of-changes-in-safety-review.md) (a change in Safety Review), [ADR-0112](0112-reconfirm-or-reject-a-flagged-booking.md) (Venue Staff settle a flag), [ADR-0127](0127-decide-a-change-request.md) (deciding a change request)

## Context
SCRUM-33 AC6 and AC7 reassess the venue bookings and withdraw a safety approval when the coordinator edits an event directly (ADR-0094). A Confirmed event can't be edited directly (ADR-0101), so the case that matters most, a Confirmed event whose date moves, arrives only through a change request (SCRUM-57). ADR-0127 applied the values and handed the equipment back, and left the rest to this story. SCRUM-58 says the same checks run when the coordinator approves a change request, and its table sets out how each booking is checked. The criteria leave open whether anything else a direct edit does also applies, and what "significant" covers.

## Decision
1. **One code path.** `_apply_and_reassess` applies the values and does everything a change unsettles, without committing: the history entries, the withdrawal, the flags, the return to Approved in Safety Review, and the equipment. `_tell_of_applied_change` sends the notices after the commit. `update_event_request` and `decide_change_request` both call them, so an edit and an approval can't drift apart. The flags' wording and the gates don't change, and neither does what flags a booking, apart from decision 9, which covers an edit too. There is no new migration, column or notification kind.
2. **A rejected request, or an approval refused as stale, reassesses nothing** (SCRUM-57 AC3, AC8). **Default: nor does an approval refused as invalid,** since re-checking the values is ADR-0127's own default. The flags, the withdrawal, the change and the decision share one commit, so a failure in any of them saves none of them.
3. **Default: "significant" is what SCRUM-33 AC2 lists, equipment requirements included.** SCRUM-58's definition names the date, start or end time, expected attendance and venue requirements, and cites SCRUM-33 AC2, which also names equipment requirements. AC6 says the rule is SCRUM-33 AC7's, and a direct edit of equipment requirements withdraws an approval, so an approved one does too: a Confirmed event goes back to Approved. The venue bookings stay untouched (AC4). This is a question for the Product Owner.
4. **Default: a Confirmed event goes back to Approved only through the withdrawal** (AC7, ADR-0087, ADR-0094 decision 11), with no status change of its own. Confirmation needs an approval and every withdrawal leaves Confirmed, so a Confirmed event always has one. A row saved straight as Confirmed with no approval, which no route can produce, would stay Confirmed.
5. **Default: SCRUM-33 AC9 applies to an approved change too.** SCRUM-58 doesn't mention it, but it runs "the same checks". In Safety Review, a flag sends the event back to Approved even with no approval to withdraw, and every Safety Officer is told, naming the fields changed.
6. **Default: nobody new is told of the return to Approved.** The Organiser gets `change_request_approved` and sees the status on the record, as ADR-0087 decision 3 leaves it; Attendees hear only of a new date or time (SCRUM-57 AC7). Registrations stay, the event leaves the catalogue until it is confirmed again (ADR-0087 decision 2), and a registered Attendee's page says its arrangements are being reviewed (ADR-0109 decision 12). Venue Staff are told per booking flagged, Technical Support once, and the coordinator who approved it isn't told (ADR-0094 decision 7).
7. **The journey goes past AC8's end.** AC8 stops at Venue Staff reconfirming. `tests/e2e/test_change_request_venue_journey.py` goes on to Technical Support confirming the reservation, a new Safety Review and a second confirmation, because that is what AC7 says must happen.
8. **The mock's approval does what its edit does.** Both go through `applyAs`. The mock takes a Confirmed event to hold an approval, since its Confirmed events have no planning record to say so. As with its edit, it raises no notices and leaves the equipment mock alone (ADR-0107 decision 10).
9. **Default: a booking an earlier rise outgrew is still judged on the next rise, by an edit too.** ADR-0094 decision 4 judged a booking only on whether its occupancy covered the attendance just before the change, so after 80 to 90 a booking left at 80 stopped counting as holding everyone: a rise to 150 added no mark, named no capacity and told nobody. AC2 marks a booking when attendance rises above its venue's capacity, AC5's table asks for reconfirmation of one that attendance rises above within capacity, and SCRUM-59's occupancy clarification names this very gap: "a booking left at 80 while attendance is 90 would stop counting as holding everyone, and a later rise would not flag it".
    - `_outgrown_by_change` now also asks, when the attendance rises, whether the booking held everyone when its period was last set (`get_event_when_arranged`, SCRUM-59's reading of the history). So 80 to 90 to 150 is No longer suitable, naming the capacity, and 80 to 90 to 95 is a further Requires reconfirmation with its own reason (ADR-0094 decision 6). Venue Staff are told each time.
    - Only a rise counts, so a fall or a change of time alone adds no attendance mark. A flag isn't what is read, so a smaller room with a timing flag stays a smaller room. Once Venue Staff reconfirm the booking, its occupancy is raised to the attendance (SCRUM-59's tenth criterion), so the normal rule applies.
    - It reads the attendance when the period was last set, so it doesn't reach a room that came to hold everyone only after it was arranged (question 2).
    - **This changes direct edits too** (SCRUM-33 AC6), because both paths call `flag_arrangements_after_change`. The mock does the same, and counts a change made while a booking was on hold, as the backend's reading of the history does.
10. **A booking On Hold isn't reassessed.** SCRUM-58 defines a venue booking as an Approved, Pending or Conflicting arrangement and narrows its first criterion to match (Product Owner's decision of 2 Nov, recorded on the story). That is what ADR-0094 decision 2 and ADR-0100 decision 2 built, and `FLAGGABLE_REQUEST_STATUSES` doesn't change. The consequence is plain: a two-venue event's hold can be given final approval unflagged, and the event can then reach **Confirmed**. Once approved, the booking is Approved and carries no mark, so the gates have nothing to refuse. Once the event is Confirmed the coordinator can't cancel the booking, unless a further significant approved change sends the event back to Approved, and cancelling then works. A change of date, time or venue requirements also marks the booking, now Approved; an attendance rise depends on the room, and any other change adds no mark. An approved change does this in three ways, with no mark on the hold and no notice about it:
    - **Date or time:** the event reaches Confirmed on 9 Dec with the booking on 2 Dec. Venue Staff are told only of the event's other booking.
    - **Attendance above capacity:** the event reaches Confirmed at 350 in a room for 270.
    - **Venue requirements:** the event reaches Confirmed without Venue Staff checking them for the hold. They are told only of the event's other booking.

    **As its text stands on 2 Nov, SCRUM-109 covers only the date or time part,** and says to decide at refinement whether to widen it. The Product Owner moved it into the active Sprint 4 on 2 Nov and is rewriting its criteria, so its description (edited at 11:51 that day) still says "Not committed to the current sprint" until that is done. This doesn't change this decision, because the definition of a venue booking puts a hold outside this story either way. SCRUM-59's "Out of scope" leaves the same part to a separate ticket. On that text, no ticket covers attendance or venue requirements on a held booking yet. `test_an_approved_change_leaves_a_booking_on_hold_unmarked_and_unannounced` pins all three as they are today.

## Consequences
- A Confirmed event's approved change of date, time, attendance, venue requirements or equipment requirements closes its registration until it passes a new Safety Review and is confirmed again.
- SCRUM-59's pre-filled period and occupancy work unchanged, because an approved change writes its `field_changed` entries as an edit does (ADR-0112's consequences).
- The SCRUM-57 test that a Confirmed event stays Confirmed after a date or time change is gone: it set the status with no approval, which can't happen.
- The event's row lock is `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` (ADR-0127 decision 2), so a venue action's history insert doesn't wait on it. The lock-order risk in ADR-0127's consequences remains; this is from reading the code, not reproduced, since it needs Postgres. An approval holds the event and then writes flags, and a flag's foreign key check waits on an arrangement another transaction read under its row lock. If that transaction then updates the event's status (`cancel_approved_booking`, `cancel_all_arrangements`, `decide_booking_request` on a replacement), one of them fails with a 500, nothing is half-saved, and a retry works. A change of date or time also marks reservations, which can meet an equipment action the same way. `record_alternative_venue_needed` (Confirmed to Approved) isn't one of these: blocking or deactivating a venue reads its arrangements unlocked, and two foreign key checks don't conflict, so it only waits for the approval's commit.

## Questions for the Product Owner
1. **Should an approved change to the equipment requirements alone send a Confirmed event back to Approved (decision 3)?** It matters because that field is free text: one changed sentence withdraws the safety approval and closes a Confirmed event's registration until it passes a new Safety Review and is confirmed again. SCRUM-58's definition leaves it out; SCRUM-33 AC2, which the definition cites, includes it.
2. A breakout room booked for 30 while the attendance is 80 isn't judged against the attendance. If the attendance falls to 25 and then rises to 50, the room is marked, because at 25 it held everyone (ADR-0094 decision 4). This is deliberate; please confirm it. Two limits come with it, and neither changes this sprint. They behave the same without decision 9, so they aren't new:
    - 80 to 25 to 50 on that room (capacity 40) marks it No longer suitable, yet reconfirming it needs no typed reason, its occupancy stays 30, and a further rise from 50 to 60 adds no mark.
    - 80 to 25 to 35 to 50 marks it only for "rose from 25 to 35" and never names the capacity.
3. Should a booking On Hold be reassessed when the attendance or the venue requirements change (decision 10)? As its text stands on 2 Nov, SCRUM-109 covers only the date or time, and leaves this to its refinement.
